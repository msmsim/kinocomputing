GPUs: the leaderboard is public, the energy numbers aren't

Thousands of simple cores doing the same operation on big arrays, which happens to be what neural-network maths needs.

Where it stands. The one independent yardstick is MLPerf, the industry's shared benchmark. Its June 2026 training round drew 95 systems on 13 kinds of accelerator. Nvidia's blog reports its GB300 systems training a 671-billion-parameter DeepSeek model in about two minutes on 8,192 GPUs. Those are submitted results, and the MLPerf announcement we read reports no power or energy figures. Memory is now as much a headline as speed: Nvidia lists 288 GB per Rubin GPU, a vendor specification, not an independent measurement.

What an outsider can use. MLPerf results and code are public on GitHub; the Zeus toolkit measures a GPU's energy per job.

What the lab could test. Energy per token against batch size and numeric precision on any rentable GPU: whether efficiency gains hold outside vendor-picked settings.
