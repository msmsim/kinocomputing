Scaling laws: bigger still helps, but shape and energy matter

The bet that a transformer's loss falls smoothly as you add parameters, data and compute, so scale itself is the strategy.

Where it stands. The scaling relationship holds in recent open experiments, but with caveats. A 2026 study of 30 designs from 17 million to 7 billion parameters, The Depth Delusion, finds that past a critical depth, adding layers raises loss despite adding parameters, so width should grow faster than depth. The OpenEuroLLM scaling study finds forms that let model and data size interact fit its open runs better than the classic separable form. On energy, Watt Counts measured over 5,000 inference runs of 50 models on 10 GPU types: the best GPU varies by model, and the choice alone can change energy use by up to 70% in some settings (its estimate). These are preprints; we read abstracts only.

What an outsider can use. Hugging Face Transformers, the OpenEuroLLM checkpoints and logs, the Watt Counts open dataset.

What the lab could test. Refit published scaling data with an energy term.
