Deep learning: energy depends on more than model size

Deep learning, the intelligence-as-many-simple-units-with-learned-connections design behind today's neural networks, has an energy bill that parameter count alone doesn't predict.

Where it stands. A US national-laboratory study, BUTTER-E, measured tens of thousands of training runs and finds energy shaped by network depth, cache behaviour and hardware, not size alone. The training system Kareus reports up to 28.3% less energy at the same training time on data-centre GPUs. Puro-2B reports pretraining a 2-billion-parameter model on consumer graphics cards for under $6,900 of compute, approaching a comparable open model under the authors' own tests. All are the authors' figures, and we read abstracts, not full papers.

What an outsider can use. PyTorch; the ML.ENERGY benchmark and its toolkit; Kareus's code.

What the lab could test. Energy per correct answer across model sizes on an ordinary CPU, where we can measure it: does a small quantised network (one with coarser stored numbers) keep its accuracy at a fraction of the energy?
