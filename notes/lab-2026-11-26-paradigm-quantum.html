Quantum computing: errors fall, no useful machine yet

Quantum computing stores information in quantum states, aiming at factoring and molecule simulation; its main obstacle is errors, corrected by combining many physical qubits into one logical one.

Where it stands. The measured news is error correction. Google reports that magic-state cultivation, a cheaper way to make the special states needed for full computation, cut errors about 40-fold on its Willow chip, with a logical fidelity of 99.99%. Also reported: dynamic surface codes that improved with larger size by 2.15 times from distance 3 to 5. A Harvard-led neutral-atom machine of up to 448 atoms is reported 2.14 times below the error threshold. These are lab-reported (we read blogs and abstracts); no one has shown a long-lived logical computer doing useful work.

What an outsider can use. IBM's free Open Plan: up to 10 minutes of QPU time per 28 days; Amazon's Braket SDK.

What the lab could test. Recompute a resource estimate under measured, not target, error rates.
