Reversible computing: energy recovery in small parts only

Reversible and adiabatic computing tries to avoid throwing away the energy of every switch: change signals slowly and return the charge to a resonating power supply, rather than dissipating it as heat.

Where it stands. Vaire's 22 nm test chip, Ice River, was reported in 2025 to recover more energy than it spends in its resonator on a capacitor array (a factor near 1.77, per the company; we did not see the data). Nothing since shows a whole processor. A September 2026 simulation of 16 nm adiabatic logic finds energy advantages over ordinary CMOS in a few small circuits, though it lists three sources of loss and nothing was fabricated. A superconducting shift register is reported to dissipate less than the Landauer limit per shift, which does not break the limit because the bits are moved, not erased (abstract only).

What an outsider can use. MQT SyReC for reversible circuit synthesis; the Adiabatic Analysis ngspice models.

What the lab could test. Sweep the power-clock ramp time in a SPICE model and see where leakage ends the gain.
