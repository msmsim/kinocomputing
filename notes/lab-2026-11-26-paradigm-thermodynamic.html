Thermodynamic computing: a million p-bits, energy unproven

Thermodynamic (or probabilistic) computing uses a chip's randomness as the engine: parts fluctuate, and the answer is read off the statistics they settle into, in the hope of cheap sampling and probabilistic maths.

Where it stands. Real hardware now exists, but the energy case is still unmeasured. A programmable machine with one million probabilistic bits, built from 18 FPGAs (reprogrammable chips), sustains 10^12 random updates a second and matches a GPU reference on optimisation runs. Four polymer-based bits sampled 16-state Bayesian inference problems within the spread expected of ideal bits. Normal Computing's digital CN101 chip is covered in our earlier note. We read the abstracts; we did not check the tables. Headline energy advantages from Extropic remain company projections as far as we found.

What an outsider can use. Extropic's open-source THRML (JAX library for Gibbs sampling) and Torx.

What the lab could test. Joules per useful sample against a GPU at equal accuracy, once any chip publishes the inputs.
