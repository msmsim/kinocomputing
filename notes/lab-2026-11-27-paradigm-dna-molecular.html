DNA computing: from kinetic circuits to equilibrium programs

DNA computing uses strands that bind, displace one another and fold, so that a test tube carries out a calculation.

Where it stands. The 2026 shift is from circuits where reactions must happen in the right order to programs whose correct answer is simply the most stable state. A Maynooth University scaffolded DNA computer (Nature, September; full text read) ran ten programs on four binding sites at about 95% yield after a three-hour anneal, and 4-bit addition in about a minute at 82%. Its 25-bit addition took up to 14 hours, with no yield in the main text; its simplest program at 25 sites reached 59%. No energy figure is reported. Soloveichik, Doty and colleagues programmed equilibrium through entropy (abstract only). Speed is nowhere near electronic.

What an outsider can use. Peppercorn and KinDA analyse strand-displacement designs; strider does thermodynamics in Python.

What the lab could test. Simulate a small scaffold program at growing size: is time to the right state set by the energy gap, or by how hard the landscape is to traverse?
