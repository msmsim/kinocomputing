DNA computing: from kinetic circuits to equilibrium programs

DNA computing uses strands that bind, displace one another and fold, so that a test tube carries out a calculation.

Where it stands. The 2026 shift is from circuits where reactions must happen in the right order to programs whose correct answer is simply the most stable state. A Trinity College Dublin-led scaffolded DNA computer (Nature, September) embeds computation, in the paper's words, "in a system relaxing to a thermodynamically favoured equilibrium state" (abstract only; we haven't read the full text or checked its program sizes). Soloveichik, Doty and colleagues programmed equilibrium through entropy, with Boolean logic. Speed is nowhere near electronic.

What an outsider can use. Peppercorn and KinDA analyse strand-displacement designs; strider does thermodynamics in Python. Outside the US, OpenAlex shows DNA computing mostly in coding theory, not wet-lab work.

What the lab could test. Simulate a small scaffold program at growing size: is time to the right state set by the energy gap, or by how hard the landscape is to traverse?
