Physical reservoir computing: tiny energy figures, thin comparisons

Physical reservoir computing lets a material's own dynamics (vibrating membranes, ion-filled nanochannels, transistors) turn a signal into a rich internal state, and trains only a simple linear readout on top.

Where it stands. A simulated fluidic iontronic circuit predicts a chaotic time series with an error near 0.06 (1.0 is the signal's own spread) at an estimated 15.8 picowatts; the authors note the comparison with earlier hardware leaves out peripheral circuitry. A 2025 edge-AI review and a 2026 memristor review both stress that low-power claims rarely count sensing, conversion and readout.

What an outsider can use. RCbench (NARMA, memory capacity), OpenPRC (mechanical reservoirs), a crumpled-paper reservoir on an Arduino and phone camera.

What the lab could test. Put a digital echo-state network, a delay reservoir and random features through one pipeline, to see how much of a physical advantage survives. Our oscillator simulation is a start.
