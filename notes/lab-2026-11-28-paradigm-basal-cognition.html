Basal cognition: learning without neurons, with the hard part undecided

Basal cognition is the study of learning-like behaviour in organisms and tissues without neurons, such as slime moulds, bacteria and embryonic cells, and the question whether that counts as a form of cognition.

Where it stands. The claims are mostly behavioural and modelled, not hardware. A 2026 study by Pla and Solé designed minimal genetic circuits that reproduce habituation, sensitisation and spacing effects. A minimal model by Sumpter's group shows how oscillation and reinforcement could let slime moulds and fungi coordinate resources. The field is still arguing over definitions: one 2026 paper proposes a mark of the noncognitive, and a 2025 paper asks how evolutionary the programme can be. Our scan flagged slime-mould maze studies and bioelectric simulators, which we have not yet checked.

What an outsider can use. Open simulators such as bioelectrics (MIT, runs on a laptop CPU) and the Levin lab's BETSE, per our scan.

What the lab could test. A foundations check of the slime-mould "Tokyo rail network" story.
