Hyperdimensional computing: lean, but not yet better than SVMs

Hyperdimensional computing (also called vector-symbolic architectures) represents everything as very long random vectors, thousands of values each, combined with a few simple operations. Learning is cheap and tolerant of noise.

Where it stands. The best controlled comparison is a 2025 review that reran many methods on seven hyperdimensional benchmarks and 121 tabular tasks: light learning, but conventional classifiers often stay more accurate on tabular data. Hardware is more credible, such as an Italian RISC-V extension for hyperdimensional operations, but headline energy figures are often design estimates for a specialised accelerator, not whole-system measurements.

Outside the US. Italy leads on open hardware (AXI-HDC-Accelerator); Italian and Swedish authors studied modular composite representations.

What an outsider can use. Torchhd, an MIT-licensed PyTorch library, runs on a laptop.

What the lab could test. A tuned SVM against hyperdimensional learning at equal memory, encoding cost counted.
