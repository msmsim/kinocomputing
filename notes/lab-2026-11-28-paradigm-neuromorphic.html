Neuromorphic computing: real wins, but only on sparse work

Neuromorphic chips imitate neurons: they pass sparse, event-driven spikes and keep memory next to computation, which can save energy when most of the input is silence.

Where it stands. The measured wins are narrow: always-on sensing, audio and event cameras. A Nature review (2025) says the field is ready to scale, and NeuroBench, a community benchmark framework, was built because comparisons with conventional hardware were not standardised. Vendor figures lean on favourable settings: Intel's Hala Point (1,152 Loihi 2 chips) is reported above 15 trillion operations per watt only on a deliberately sparse network.

Outside the US. SpiNNaker (Manchester, Dresden), Innatera (Netherlands) and SynSense (Zurich) build chips; the Netherlands also produced a complexity theory for spiking networks.

What an outsider can use. Free software: NeuroBench, Lava and the NIR interchange format. Hardware is by application or quote.

What the lab could test. Whether a spiking network's energy edge survives once preprocessing and idle power are counted.

Checked 28 November 2026; first pass. Chip claims are from vendors and reviews, not our measurements.
