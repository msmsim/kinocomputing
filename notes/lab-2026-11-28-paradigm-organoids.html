Organoid intelligence: the tissue's measured contribution is small so far

Organoid intelligence means growing small clumps of human brain cells, called organoids, on electrode arrays and using their electrical responses to stimulation for computation.

Where it stands. In a 2026 University of Bristol study, three organoids decoded 26 Braille letters at 83%, but the same classifier reading the stimulation pattern directly scored 82%. They scored better when inputs were corrupted, though the comparison was not like for like. A 2026 benchmarking framework argues the field lacks standard qualification of organoids and interfaces, and that results vary between preparations. Energy claims rest on a brain-versus-supercomputer comparison in the 2023 roadmap, not measurement.

Outside the US. Bristol (UK) did the Braille work on FinalSpark's platform (Switzerland). UK grant searches found organoid disease models, not computing programmes.

What an outsider can use. FinalSpark's Neuroplatform offers remote organoid access (price on request); NeuroBridge (MIT) analyses recordings.

What the lab could test. Stimulus-only against response classifiers on a public recording.
