Wetware computing: living neurons do tasks, but who does the work?

Synthetic biological intelligence grows neurons or small brain-like organoids on electrode arrays and tries to use them as computers, by stimulating them and reading their activity.

Where it stands. Results are real but narrow. Neuron cultures have shown altered network activity in closed-loop tasks such as the Pong game, and a 2025 Braille-recognition study with organoids (FinalSpark and Bristol) classified tactile patterns. The open worry is attribution: much of the performance can come from the stimulation code and the conventional classifier that reads the signals, not from the tissue.

What an outsider can use. FinalSpark's Neuroplatform sells remote access to living organoids with a Python interface; reported pricing was about $1,000 a month in 2025, to be confirmed. Cortical Labs' CL1, a purchasable biological computer, was reported at $35,000.

Outside the US. FinalSpark is in Switzerland and the Braille work includes Bristol, UK.

What the lab could test. Train the same classifier on the stimulus alone and on the recorded response, to see what the tissue adds.
